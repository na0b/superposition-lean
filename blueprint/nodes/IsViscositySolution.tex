\begin{definition}[Viscosity solution of $L(\nabla u,\nabla^2u)=f$, paper Def.~def-vis]
Let $n\ge0$, $\Omega\subseteq\mathbb R^n$, $L:\mathbb R^n\times\mathbb R^{n\times n}\to\mathbb R$ and $f,u:\mathbb R^n\to\mathbb R$. We say that $u$ is a \emph{viscosity solution of $L(\nabla u,\nabla^2u)=f$ in $\Omega$} if $u$ is both a viscosity subsolution (\noderef{IsViscositySubsolution}) and a viscosity supersolution (\noderef{IsViscositySupersolution}) of $L(\nabla u,\nabla^2u)=f$ in $\Omega$. Since the first requires $u$ to be upper and the second lower semicontinuous on $\Omega$, $u$ is then continuous on $\Omega$, i.e.\ $u\in C(\Omega)$, matching the paper's definition of a viscosity solution as a function $u\in C(\Omega)$ that is both a viscosity subsolution and a viscosity supersolution.
\end{definition}
