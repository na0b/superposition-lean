\begin{definition}[Geodesic space]
A metric space $(\mathbf X, d_{\mathbf X})$ is a \emph{geodesic space} if for any two points $x, y\in \mathbf X$ there is a map $\gamma:\mathbb R\to \mathbf X$ with $\gamma(0)=x$, $\gamma(d_{\mathbf X}(x,y))=y$, and
\[
d_{\mathbf X}(\gamma(s),\gamma(t))=|s-t|\qquad\text{for all } s,t\in[0,d_{\mathbf X}(x,y)],
\]
that is, $x$ and $y$ are joined by a geodesic segment (an isometric copy of the interval $[0,d_{\mathbf X}(x,y)]$). (The values of $\gamma$ outside $[0,d_{\mathbf X}(x,y)]$ play no role.)
\end{definition}
